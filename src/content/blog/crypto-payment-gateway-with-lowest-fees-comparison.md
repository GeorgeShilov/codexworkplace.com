# Crypto payment gateway with lowest fees 2026 comparison

When a business starts accepting digital assets, the first question is rarely about wallets or blockchains. It is about cost. A checkout that swallows two or three percent of every order quietly erodes margin, and the effect compounds once volume grows. Choosing a <a href="https://nowpayments.io">bitcoin payment gateway</a> is therefore a cost decision as much as a technical one, and the cheapest headline rate is not always the cheapest option in practice.

This comparison breaks down how gateway pricing actually works, which fee models exist, and how to estimate the real cost for a merchant of a given size.

## The three fee models you will encounter

Almost every provider in the market prices using one of three structures:

1. **Flat percentage per transaction.** Predictable, easy to model, and usually between 0.4% and 1% for crypto settlements.
2. **Tiered volume pricing.** The rate falls as monthly volume rises, which rewards scaling but makes forecasting harder early on.
3. **Network fee pass-through plus a service margin.** The provider charges the blockchain cost at cost and adds a fixed service fee on top.

The subtlety is that "network fee" is not constant. It depends on the chain, the token standard, and how congested the mempool is at the moment of the transaction.

## Comparing the models side by side

| Model | Typical cost | Best for | Main drawback |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flat percentage | 0.4% – 1.0% | Stable, high-volume retail | No discount as volume grows |
| Tiered volume | 0.8% → 0.2% | Marketplaces, SaaS billing | Hard to forecast at low volume |
| Network pass-through + margin | 0.2% – 0.5% + gas | Merchants comfortable with volatility | Cost spikes during congestion |

A merchant processing $50,000 a month will usually find the flat model the simplest. Above roughly $250,000 a month, tiered pricing starts to pay off. The pass-through model suits teams that already understand on-chain costs and want to optimise them directly.

## Why the lowest headline percentage can still lose

A 0.2% rate looks unbeatable until you examine what it excludes. Watch for:

- **Withdrawal fees.** Some providers charge nothing to accept but take a cut to convert to fiat or move funds off-platform.
- **Settlement spreads.** If the gateway converts crypto to fiat at an unfavourable internal rate, the hidden cost can exceed the stated fee.
- **Minimum payouts.** High thresholds can trap small balances that never clear the withdrawal minimum.
- **Inactive or maintenance fees.** Rare, but they exist and are easy to miss.

The honest way to compare is to model total cost of ownership over a representative month, not to sort providers by their advertised percentage.

## Settlement speed and finality

Cost is not the only lever. A payment is only useful once it is settled and confirmed. Gateways differ in how they handle confirmations:

- **Instant-authorised, later-settled** gateways credit the order immediately and assume confirmation risk themselves.
- **Confirmation-gated** gateways wait for a set number of blocks, which protects the merchant but slows fulfilment.

For digital goods, instant authorisation matters. For physical shipping, a confirmation threshold is usually acceptable. The right choice depends on chargeback exposure and fraud risk in your category.

## Liquidity and supported assets

The number of supported coins is a marketing metric, not a business metric. What matters is liquidity for the assets your customers actually hold. A gateway that lists hundreds of obscure tokens but has thin liquidity on the major ones will cost you more through spreads than it saves on fees.

Regulatory posture matters too. The <a href="https://www.bis.org/publ/work1049.htm" rel="nofollow">Bank for International Settlements</a> has documented how settlement finality and counterparty risk differ across payment rails, and those principles apply to crypto gateways as much as to traditional systems.

## How to run a fair comparison

1. **Benchmark one full month of real traffic.** Use actual order sizes and asset mix, not averages.
2. **Include every cost.** Acceptance, conversion, withdrawal, and network fees.
3. **Check settlement timing.** Test a small live transaction and time the fiat arrival.
4. **Review the integration cost.** A cheaper gateway that takes two weeks of engineering effort may not be cheaper overall.

Merchants who treat gateway selection as a one-time decision tend to overpay. Re-running the comparison annually, or whenever volume doubles, keeps pricing aligned with reality.

## A practical checklist

- Confirm the fee for your actual asset mix, not the best-case one.
- Ask what happens during congestion: who absorbs the gas spike?
- Verify the settlement currency and the conversion rate applied.
- Read the withdrawal and inactivity terms before signing.
- Test the API and dashboard with a small live payment.

## Bottom line

The gateway with the lowest fees is the one whose *total* cost is lowest at your volume, for your asset mix, with your settlement needs. A flat 0.5% with fast fiat settlement often beats a 0.2% rate with hidden conversions. Model the real numbers, test with live traffic, and revisit the decision as you scale.
